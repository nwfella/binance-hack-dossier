Five things, in order of urgency. The full analysis and the technical forensics are in the PDF.

Do notPanic-withdraw to an unverified destination

  • Stuck in a queue is recoverable. Sent to the wrong chain usually is not.
  • Send a small test amount first — one extra fee for real protection.
  • Confirm the specific network is open on Binance's status page; the headline statement is authoritative, per-network indicators lag.

AssumeYou are now a phishing target

  • Expect fake “Binance recovery”, “verify to unlock withdrawal” emails, SMS, DMs and paid search ads.
  • Binance will never DM first or ask for a password, 2FA code or seed phrase.
  • Navigate by typing the domain or using the app. Never via a link in a message.

CheckWhere your assets actually sit

  • Spot, Earn/staking, futures margin and Binance Alpha are different claims with different unwind mechanics.
  • Know which bucket you're in before you need to move.
  • Don't add leverage on the venue you're worried about.

AuditEIP-7702 delegations on your own wallets

  • An EOA can now be running someone else's code. Check every address you control for unexpected delegations.
  • Clear the delegation first, then rotate keys, then move funds.
  • Tools: Etherscan “delegated” indicators, Revoke.cash.

02 Incident brief

The short version, revised as Binance's own findings have landed. Section 06 has the full timeline.

A compromised engineer's laptop — not a leaked private key

Binance links the theft to a DevOps engineer's compromised laptop that gave access to a service container and let the attacker submit unauthorised withdrawals. Binance found no evidence of a leaked wallet private key, later disclosed "additional points of breach", and co-CEO Richard Teng stressed it was not one engineer's permissions or a single click. EIP-7702 did not cause the breach — it amplified it.

The attack began with XRP, ten minutes early

Between 20:05 and 20:09 UTC, before anything else moved, five Binance wallets on the XRP Ledger sent 233.46M XRP (~$296M) to a single attacker address. Bitcoin, Tron, Solana and Optimism followed by 20:22; the Ethereum sweep at 20:24 was the biggest single move, not the first one. Preparation began at least 36 hours earlier.

Then a 50-authorisation nonce spray

Because Binance 14's nonce was advancing with daily payouts, the attacker signed 50 EIP-7702 authorisations across a contiguous nonce window in one type-4 transaction. Forty-nine failed, one matched. The delegated wallet swept 167,464 ETH and 63 tokens, then swept again at 20:41, 20:59, 21:04, 21:44, 22:39 and 23:56 UTC.

Counterparty risk, realised — then partly cured

The drained wallets were the ones Binance publishes as proof-of-reserves backing. Binance has since set aside $6.34B, replaced ~90%, declared ten major assets 100% backed, and is buying the rest via market and OTC. The new zero-knowledge proof takes 40–50 hours, so customer verification lands around 3 Nov — not immediately.

06 Timeline

All times UTC. The attack began on 29 October; the corporate response ran through 1 November. Sequence reconstructed from TRM Labs, Blockaid and Binance's own updates.

28 Oct, morning (T−36h)

Financing. An attacker-controlled wallet receives three small transfers from Tornado Cash. Preparation runs at least 36 hours.

29 Oct, early

Rehearsal. Malicious contract code is deployed and tested against two fresh Ethereum wallets, then a third, with test funds moved and returned. All of it visible on-chain in advance.

20:05–20:09 (first move)

XRP goes first. Five Binance wallets on the XRP Ledger send 233.46M XRP (~$296M) to a single attacker address — ten minutes before every other chain. This was the canary in the coal mine.

20:10

Transfers begin from Binance wallets on Solana and Optimism.

20:20 (16:20 ET)

Bitcoin. 18,270 BTC (~$1.47B) leaves a Binance wallet, immediately split into 1,000 and 100 BTC batches. Simultaneously the attacker submits 50 EIP-7702 authorisations to Binance 14 — 49 invalid, 1 matches.

20:22

Tron. 612.7M USDT and 46.4M TRX (~$628M) move to new addresses.

20:24:23

The Ethereum sweep. 167,464 ETH, 1.615B USDT and 63 tokens in one transaction (~$2.5B). Binance 14 is left holding 1 ETH. Twenty-four seconds later Binance 16 sends 28,005.6 ETH more.

20:41 → 23:56

Standing drain. Further sweeps at 20:41, 20:59, 21:04, 21:44, 22:39 and 23:56 as fresh assets land in the delegated wallet. It also exercises permissions over a separate Binance smart account, moving another 823M USDC.

~21:04 (17:04 ET)

Freezes begin. Tether freezes 975.1M USDT, Circle 823M USDC. Ripple, World Liberty Financial, Paxos and the Arbitrum Security Council follow.

22:22 (18:22 ET)

Binance pauses all deposits and withdrawals, having earlier paused Solana, XRP and several stablecoin networks.

23:05 (second wave)

The operation extends to further Binance wallets on Avalanche and Polygon.

00:30, 30 Oct

08 How this page works

Zero AI cost

No LLM calls, no API keys, no metered inference. A plain Python script polls the public RSS feeds above, filters for this incident, deduplicates, and rebuilds this page hourly at :05 from a scheduled task, with a free GitHub Actions run as a fallback when the host machine is offline.

No runtime fetch

The data is baked into the HTML at build time, so the page renders fully even where XHR/fetch to github.io is blocked by a corporate proxy. Nothing loads from a third party when you open it.

Read the caveats

Headlines are reproduced as-is, not summarised — including ones that report unverified numbers. The PDF is where claims are weighed and attributed.

Method. Compiled from primary reporting and on-chain analysis published 29–30 October 2026. Where independent parties disagree, both figures are shown with attribution rather than averaged. Claims sourced only to social media are labelled as such. Transaction hashes and addresses are reproduced so you can verify rather than trust.

Bias notice. Early outflow figures skew high (gross tallies include transfers later found not to be attacker-initiated, and get revised down); company statements skew reassuring (“no confirmed impact to user funds” was Binance's line for hours while funds were demonstrably leaving its wallets).

Not advice. This is a research summary, not a forensic audit or financial, legal or investment advice. Binance has not published a post-mortem; nothing here is a finding of fact about its solvency, its controls, or the attacker's identity.
